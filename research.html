Research

Everyday rhythms, material lives, and worlds in translation.

Research overview

Literature across bodies, objects, and borders.

Working at the intersections of Victorian literary studies, gender and sexuality studies, material culture, and transnational literary history, I examine forms of agency and identity that become visible through everyday practices. My projects trace how people navigate the social expectations, physical environments, and linguistic frameworks that structure their lives.

Across these areas, I am interested in what happens when we attend to the seemingly ordinary: habits of movement, relationships with objects, diaries, and translations.

01 / BOOK PROJECT
Victorian literatureGender and material culture

Reclaiming (Lost) Rhythms in Victorian Female Narratives

My first book project examines how female characters in Victorian literature negotiate the rhythms imposed by domestic life, social conventions, and gendered expectations. It considers how relationships with material objects and everyday practices reveal forms of agency that may be overlooked when women's lives are read primarily through conventional narratives of resistance or confinement.

Bringing together nineteenth-century British fiction and its later literary afterlives, the project explores how movement, repetition, and material environments shape the possibilities of female self-determination.

02 / BOOK PROJECT
Transimperial historyTranslation and diplomacy

Trans-(Nation)-lating and Subverting the Linguistic Empire

My second book project investigates nineteenth-century encounters between Britain and East Asia through translation, travel writing, and diplomatic representation. It asks how language mediates imperial authority and how translated texts may resist, complicate, or unsettle the assumptions of those who interpret them.

Current case studies include the British interpretation of Lin Changyi's poem “Shooting the Eagle,” the contrasting diplomatic travelogues of Robert Hart and Liu Hsi-hung, and representations of the Korean court dancer Li Tsin. Together, these materials reveal the uneven circulation of texts, cultural values, and knowledge across imperial borders.

03 / DIGITAL HUMANITIES
Urban space and mobility

The New York Subway Project

This digital humanities project explores how the New York City subway becomes a site for thinking about race, mobility, labor, and urban belonging. It brings literary and cultural analysis into conversation with the material infrastructures that shape movement through the city.
