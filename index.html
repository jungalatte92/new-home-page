Rhythms, movement,
and worlds in translation.

I am a Marion L. Brittain Postdoctoral Fellow at the Georgia Institute of Technology. My research examines how literary narratives, material objects, and practices of translation mediate experiences of gender, mobility, and empire across nineteenth-century Britain and East Asia.

Research

Literature, material culture, and global encounters.

My work brings together Victorian literary studies, East Asian cultural history, and digital humanities to examine how people, objects, and texts circulate across borders.

Reclaiming (Lost) Rhythms in Victorian Female Narratives

My first book project investigates how women in Victorian literature negotiate dominant social rhythms through their relationships with material objects.

Learn more →

Transimperial Translation and Diplomatic Encounters

My current research examines nineteenth-century encounters between Britain and China, focusing on Robert Hart, Liu Hsi-hung, diary writing, and translation.

Literature, writing, and ethical communication.

I design courses that connect literary study, critical inquiry, and real-world communication.
